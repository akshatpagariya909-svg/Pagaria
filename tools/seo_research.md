# What architects and students search for, and what Discover Architecture can do about it

> **Audience correction (15 Oct 2026, owner's rule).** The audience is global, and the Western countries (USA, UK, Europe, Canada, Australia) are the biggest target. This research was done with **India-only** Google data (autocomplete, Trends) and so over-weights India: read its India findings as one market among many, not as the plan. The method (what people type after a building's name, what they get, the gap) holds everywhere. Still to do: repeat the autocomplete, Trends and result-page checks for the US, UK, Germany, France, Canada and Australia, and decide the priority list from those. Needs network access, so it runs on GitHub Actions (data-collection chat).

*12 October 2026. Sources: Google autocomplete (India, ~450 seed queries + 137 building names), Google Trends (India, 5 years), Wikipedia pageviews for 796 wall buildings and 45 architects (Oct 2025–Sep 2026), Bing results for sample queries (Google blocked automated result pages), published studies of student search behaviour, and a technical check of the live site.*

## The three-line verdict

1. **Students don't search for "buildings"; they search for deliverables: "plan", "section", "case study", "sheets", "pdf", "sketch".** Nobody authoritative serves these for Indian buildings. The results are Scribd PDFs, SlideShare decks, Pinterest boards and YouTube videos.
2. **A building's name is searched by two audiences at once:** visitors (photos, tickets, reviews, timings, location) and students (plan, case study, architect, section). Wikipedia wins the first, nobody wins the second. Your building page should serve both on one URL.
3. **Interest is extremely concentrated:** the top 10 of your 796 buildings get 28% of all Wikipedia views; the top 100 get 68%. Rank, link and write for those first. Among Indian buildings, Lotus Temple (176k views/yr) gets more attention than Sangath, Kanchanjunga and IIM-A combined, and those three are hidden behind name collisions.

### 1b. Student seed queries (India)

- *architecture case study* → sheets, websites, presentation, ppt, examples, template, sheet presentation, checklist, pdf
- *architecture students* → portfolio, laptop, gifts, drawing kit, instagram, website, books, software, internship
- *site analysis* (Trends, rising) → "what is site analysis", "site analysis pdf", "site analysis book", "google earth"
- *famous buildings* (Trends, rising) → "le corbusier famous buildings" +90%, "top 10 famous buildings in the world" +80%, "zaha hadid famous buildings" +50%
- *how to analyse a building*, *precedent study*, *architecture concept*: suggestions are generic how-to and "examples" / "pdf".

The pattern: students search for the **format of the assignment** (sheets, ppt, pdf, template, checklist) more than for the building. The building is a means to the sheet.

## 2. What they get

Bing results (Google's result pages blocked automation; Bing's top results for these queries are the same sites):

| Query | Who ranks | What's wrong with it |
|---|---|---|
| villa savoye plan | ArchEyes, WikiArquitectura, Scribd PDF, dwgLAB, Archweb, ArchDaily, a WordPress PDF | Drawings scattered across six sites; Scribd gates downloads; no one page has photo + plan + section + why it matters |
| architecture case study sheets | Rethinking The Future checklist, Scribd, Template.net, Pinterest, SlideShare, Issuu, YouTube (50–120k views) | Format advice without the content; students copy other students' sheets |
| brutalist buildings in india | Bing AI answer, MagicBricks (real-estate site), Novatr (ed-tech blog), RTF | No architecture source owns the query |
| sangath bv doshi plan section | WikiArquitectura, Scribd, SlideShare, ArchDaily AD Classics (2011), YouTube case studies | Best page is 15 years old; drawings are in student uploads |

Context from published work:
- Makri & Warwick (2010) and later studies: students search to be *inspired*, not to find exact answers; they use Google Images and Pinterest ahead of databases, and over-rely on precedents as models to copy.
- ArchDaily: ~7.3M visits/month (Semrush, Sep 2026), 51% direct, 36% from Google; traffic down ~30% year on year in Semrush's data as AI Overviews answer "who designed X / when" directly. Facts are being commoditised; drawings, curated comparison and point of view are not.
- Gen Z: roughly 40–45% say they search social platforms first (2024 survey), but a 2026 study shows Google still ranked most helpful by 85%. Instagram/Pinterest are discovery; Google is still where the assignment gets done.

## 3. The gap, in one table

| Students want | What exists | Who could own it |
|---|---|---|
| Plan + section + photo + "why it matters" for one building on one page | Scattered across 4–6 sites, often as PDFs | A building page that already has the photo, the study line, and links to drawings |
| Case-study-ready material for *Indian* precedents | Student SlideShares, 2011 ArchDaily classics | An Indian-first wall with 49 South Asian buildings already |
| "Buildings like this one" / precedents by idea (courtyard, pilotis, jali, vault) | Pinterest boards | Your `concepts` field and "Find more with" |
| What to see, when it's open, can you photograph it (the visitor half of the same query) | Google Maps, TripAdvisor, random blogs | A line of practical info on the same building page |
| Famous buildings by architect ("le corbusier famous buildings") | Listicles | Architect pages that already exist on the site |

## 4. Your site today (technical)

Good: unique title per building ("Villa Savoye, Le Corbusier · Discover Architecture"), meta description, canonical, Open Graph image, JSON-LD `LandmarksOrHistoricalBuildings`, sitemap with 1,605 URLs, robots.txt.

Weak:
- **Thin pages:** 318–368 words of visible text on a building page; the home page renders 67 words without JavaScript. Google can index it, but a page with one line of "why it matters" won't outrank a 1,500-word ArchDaily classic.
- **Not indexed yet:** DuckDuckGo and Bing returned no results for `site:akshatpagariya909-svg.github.io/Pagaria`. Submit the sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools this week; nothing else matters until that's done.
- **Titles don't carry the query words:** "plan", "section", "case study" appear nowhere in titles or descriptions. Where the page links to drawings, say so in the title: "Villa Savoye — plans, sections and photos · Le Corbusier, 1931".
- **Name-collision buildings** need the architect in the title (Sangath, IIM-A, CEPT, Mill Owners', Golconde).
- **Brand collision:** discoverarch.org calls itself "Discover Architecture", runs Indian student competitions (claims 5,000+ students, 250+ institutions), and owns @discoverarch on Instagram. Searches for "discover architecture" will show them first for a long time. Decide now whether to live with that, differentiate hard ("Discover Architecture · the wall"), or rename before the Instagram page goes live.

## 5. What to do: in priority order

**Now, no new features**
1. Submit the sitemap to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
2. Rewrite building titles and descriptions to carry the words people add: *plan, section, photos, case study, architect, city*. Keep the site's own short text; this is metadata, not body copy.
3. On the top 100 buildings by pageviews, add 150–250 words of real content per page: the "Key features" line expanded into 3–4 short paragraphs (site, plan, section, material). This is the single biggest ranking lever and stays within "no invented facts" if sourced from Wikipedia/Commons descriptions.
4. Give the home page a server-rendered intro (two sentences and a list of the 20 most-searched buildings) so it isn't 67 words to a crawler.

**Next: features that match the search data, consistent with the site's rules**
5. **"Study it" block on every building page:** plan, section, elevation thumbnails when Commons has them (Roark shows 600+ exist), each credited and linked. This is the "plan / section / sketch" demand and your Commons-only rule allows it. Where Commons has no drawing, link out and say so.
6. **One page, two audiences:** a short "Visiting" line (city, open to public or not, map link, photography allowed?) on the same page. The data says tickets/timings/location are asked as often as plan. Cheap, sourced from Wikidata/official sites, and it pulls visitor traffic to a page that then shows students' material.
7. **Case-study starter, not a template:** a "Print this building" view that lays out photo, drawings, facts, key features and sources on one A3-style page, credits intact. Students search "case study sheets"; give them the sourced raw material in the shape they need, without pretending to be their sheet.
8. **Precedents by idea:** promote "Find more with" into landing pages per concept ("Courtyard", "Pilotis", "Jali", "Vault", "Brise-soleil"), each a wall of 20–40 buildings. These match "precedent study" and "concept" searches and nothing like them exists for Indian work.
9. **Architect pages with drawings-first galleries** for the names people search in India: Le Corbusier, Correa, Baker, Doshi, Bawa, Kahn, Rewal (add Raj Rewal's buildings: Hall of Nations, Asian Games Village, Parliament Library), Kanvinde, Tabassum.
10. **"Compare two buildings"**: pick two from the wall, see plans at the same scale side by side. No competitor offers it; it is exactly how studio teaches precedent.

**Later: outside the box, still inside the rules**
11. **Search by sketch or photo** ("looks like this"): Roark does it with image embeddings; you could use Commons' depicts data and your concept tags without any ML to start.
12. **City walks**: "Ahmedabad in a day: 8 buildings, in order", "Chandigarh Capitol in two hours". The data shows city + architecture queries and visitor intents; students travel for studio trips.
13. **Semester calendar content**: "case study" interest peaks Jan–Mar and Aug–Sep. Publish the concept pages and the Indian case-study starters a month before each peak.
14. **The Hindi gap is not drawings**, it's school essays and home plans. Skip it, except a glossary of plan/section/elevation terms in Hindi if you want goodwill traffic.

## 6. How to measure after launch

Search Console (free) will tell you, per page, which queries bring clicks. Watch three things: impressions for "<building> plan/case study" queries, click-through on the top-100 pages, and whether Indian buildings start appearing for their own names without the architect's. Re-run the autocomplete and pageview scripts quarterly; they take 20 minutes.

## Method notes and limits

- Google autocomplete reflects what Google chooses to suggest, not raw volume; counts above are "number of buildings/seeds where the word appears", not searches per month. Keyword-tool volumes (Semrush, Ahrefs) need paid accounts and disagree with each other by 5–10×.
- Google blocked automated result pages after two queries; result-page evidence comes from Bing with India settings. For these queries Bing's top sites matched what Google is known to show (ArchDaily, WikiArquitectura, Scribd, Pinterest, YouTube).
- Wikipedia pageviews measure general public interest in English; they undercount Indian student interest in buildings without good articles (Sangath, Kanchanjunga, Aranya, CDS).
- The US autocomplete pass was still running when this was written; India is the audience, and the India pass is complete.
