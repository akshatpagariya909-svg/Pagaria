# Discover Architecture: built for architecture students

**The problem.** To look up a building today, you already have to know its name or its architect. Nothing lets a student *stumble onto* buildings and then follow links to read, see drawings and go deeper.

**Who it's for first.** Architecture students: first-years building a vocabulary, studio students hunting precedents, and thesis students researching an idea. It's not limited to them later.

## What students actually come for

1. **Precedents for studio.** "Show me courtyard housing in a hot climate", "a museum lit from above", "a school built from local material". They search by **idea and problem**, not by name.
2. **The canon they're taught.** Le Corbusier, Mies, Kahn, Aalto, Wright, then Ando, Zumthor, SANAA, OMA, Zaha Hadid, BIG, Doshi, Correa, Kéré. Mostly 1920 to today.
3. **Drawings, not just photos.** Plans, sections, axonometrics and models. These are mostly copyrighted, so we **link to where they are**: ArchDaily, WikiArquitectura, Divisare and the architects' own sites.
4. **Things to quote.** A one-line reason the building matters, the architect's idea in a few words, and the facts needed for a citation.
5. **Range.** Indian, Japanese, Latin American and African modernism alongside Europe and the US, plus small and cheap buildings, not only icons.

## What changes on the site

| | Before | For students |
|---|---|---|
| Buildings | Mostly famous and historic | Mostly 1920–today: masters, brutalism, regionalism, contemporary, parametric |
| Images | 50% paintings, prints, archive | Mostly photographs. Free drawings where they exist (e.g. US government HABS drawings), plus a **"See drawings"** link on every building |
| Main filter | Medium, era, region | **Concept** (courtyard, promenade, brise-soleil, cantilever, exposed concrete, brick, light from above, adaptive reuse, parametric skin…), then typology, architect, country, decade |
| Text per building | Facts only | Facts + 3 concept tags + one line: **"Study it for:"** (e.g. *the five points of architecture in one house*) |
| Links | Wikipedia, ArchDaily, Commons, Arts & Culture | **Drawings** (WikiArquitectura / ArchDaily), Read (Wikipedia), Projects (ArchDaily / Dezeen), Architect's site, Video |

## Image rights: one catch

France has no "freedom of panorama", so Wikimedia Commons can't host free photos of copyrighted French buildings: **Villa Savoye, Unité d'Habitation, Ronchamp, La Tourette, Centre Pompidou, Maison de Verre**. There are similar gaps for some Italian buildings. The options are:
- keep them with a drawing-style placeholder and strong outbound links,
- license photos (paid or by permission),
- or ask the firms and foundations. Students and educators often get permission.
