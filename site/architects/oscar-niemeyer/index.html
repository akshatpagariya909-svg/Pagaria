Architect

Oscar Niemeyer

1907–2012 · Brazilian · 35 buildings on the wall

Oscar Niemeyer (1907–2012) was a Brazilian architect and a key figure of modern architecture, known for free, flowing curves in reinforced concrete. He designed the main civic buildings of Brasília, the planned city that became Brazil's capital in 1960, and won the Pritzker Prize in 1988.

Read moreShow less

Niemeyer said his architecture really began at Pampulha, near Belo Horizonte, where the Church of Saint Francis of Assisi (1943) is built from parabolic concrete arches. He worked with Lúcio Costa on the Gustavo Capanema Palace in Rio de Janeiro, and with Le Corbusier and others on the United Nations Secretariat Building in New York. The Copan Building in São Paulo wraps its apartments in a long, wavy façade. In Brasília, the National Congress of Brazil sets two half-spheres, one for each chamber, beside twin office towers, and the Cathedral of Brasília rises as a ring of sixteen curved concrete columns with glass between them. At 89 he designed the Niterói Contemporary Art Museum (1996), a saucer on a cylindrical base above the bay. Oscar Niemeyer's buildings show students how far concrete can be shaped.
