Architect

Alvar Aalto

1898–1976 · Finnish · 29 buildings on the wall

Alvar Aalto (1898–1976) was a Finnish architect and designer who gave modernism a human scale through wood, brick, daylight and curving lines. His career runs from Nordic Classicism in the 1920s, through functionalism in the 1930s, to the organic modernism of his later work. With his first wife, Aino Aalto, he also designed furniture, lamps and glassware, and their bent plywood furniture influenced Charles and Ray Eames.

The Paimio Sanatorium (1933), designed with Aino Aalto, and Viipuri Library in Vyborg (1935) first won him world attention; the library has an undulating timber ceiling shaped to spread sound and deep round skylights that light the reading desks without glare. At Villa Mairea in Noormarkku (1939), stair columns of bound wood suggest a forest. Baker House at MIT in Cambridge, Massachusetts (1949), bends along the Charles River to give each room a view and fresh air, and began his red-brick period. Säynätsalo Town Hall (1952) gathers a council chamber, offices and a library around a raised courtyard in brick, timber and copper. Alvar Aalto's buildings are studied for natural materials, daylight, and designing a building and its furnishings as one.
